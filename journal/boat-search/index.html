The Search for a Home That Sails

Months of research, inspections, and decisions · November 2026

Over the last several months I’ve been looking for a boat I can live aboard, sail mostly alone, take offshore, and still work from as a momentum day trader.

The Tayana 37 has been the center of the search. The process included reading, broker calls, several personal inspections, two trips to the same boat, a signed purchase agreement, and ultimately walking away after the survey revealed problems I wasn’t willing to take on.

What matters

I’m 6′2″, so headroom and sleeping space are essential. I prefer a cutter rig, something under 44 feet, a dependable engine and rigging, and a dry interior. I can handle maintenance, but I don’t want a major restoration project. The boat also needs room and power for a trading workstation — Starlink first, then dual monitors and a reliable laptop.

A respected design or claimed refit cannot substitute for a dry interior, sound structure, and verified maintenance.

The boats I inspected

Four in-person visits across three different Tayanas. Two of them were rejected after a single look. One went further.

Rejected

Three Little Birds

Cambridge, MD · Interior rot, leaks, neglected systems. Ruled out after inspection.

Rejected

Briana

Reedville, VA · Diesel odor, deferred maintenance, uncertainty on engine and rigging.

Pantala

A 1982 Tayana 37 in New Bern, North Carolina. She looked more finished than the others, so she became the leading candidate. I drove overnight from New Jersey for the first visit, then flew back for the survey.

The second trip exposed more serious issues: bowsprit and bulkhead work needed, a smoking engine, diesel odor throughout the cabin, chainplate leaks, and an incomplete haul-out. The survey stopped early when the captain refused to move the boat to the yard.

I formally rejected the boat in early November. The deposit was returned. Pantala was over.

Where it stands now

Teki Malo, a 1988 Tayana 37 in Río Dulce, became a serious prospect after a remote walkthrough. Purchase paperwork is active and an inspection trip is planned for later in November. Scrimshaw, a 1979 Baba 35 in the Dominican Republic, is also under consideration after a remote showing.
